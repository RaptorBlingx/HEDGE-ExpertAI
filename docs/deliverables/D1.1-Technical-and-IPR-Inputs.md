## 1. How to use this input pack

This is not a completed D1.1 and does not make unverified legal claims. It separates information into three evidence classes:

- **Confirmed:** directly evidenced by the audited repository snapshot or an authoritative upstream source.
- **Proposal declaration:** stated in the proposal file, but not independently proven by repository evidence.
- **Open decision/evidence:** must be supplied or approved by the project manager, consortium, legal counsel, infrastructure owner, or data owner.

The manager can use the confirmed material as technical input. Items labelled open must not be silently converted into facts in D1.1.

## 2. What D1.1 needs beyond a technology list

The Project Management & IPR Handbook should normally establish:

1. document control, approval authority, versioning, review, and change history;
2. project roles, responsibilities, named contacts, decision rights, escalation, and a RACI or equivalent;
3. work plan, milestones, deliverables, review/acceptance workflow, reporting calendar, and dependencies;
4. meeting and communication cadence, minutes, action tracking, consortium interfaces, and repository/document locations;
5. quality assurance, configuration/change management, testing evidence, risk/issue management, and corrective actions;
6. background-IP register and access rights required during and after the project;
7. foreground/results ownership, contributor assignment, joint-ownership rules if applicable, and invention disclosure;
8. open-source policy, inbound dependency approval, outbound licensing, notices, source-offer obligations where applicable, and SBOM maintenance;
9. model, dataset, documentation, standards, API, trademark, and other non-code rights;
10. confidentiality, publication/dissemination review, patent-vs-publication timing, and exploitation rights;
11. data protection, ethics, security, retention, lawful basis, controller/processor roles, incident handling, and DPIA triggers;
12. infrastructure ownership, third-party services, procurement/subscription terms, credentials, backup, continuity, and end-of-project handover.
13. budget/effort control, timesheets, eligible-cost evidence, procurement/subcontracting approvals, financial reporting, and audit-record retention;
14. contractual dissemination obligations, including HEDGE/EU acknowledgement, emblem, funding statement, disclaimer, publication review, and file-level dissemination classification.

The repository supplies only part of this information. Sections 11 and 12 list the declarations, decisions, and evidence still needed.

## 9. Existing-disclosure review and outbound release procedure for D1.1

Because the audited branch is already public, the manager should first perform and document a retrospective review of the exposed code, models/configuration references, data, prompts, examples, proposal, and internal-marked reports. That review should determine authorization, file-level licence/dissemination status, confidentiality or personal-data exposure, patent implications, branding permission, and any required remediation.

For every further public or consortium release, the handbook should require:

1. freeze a release commit and build from clean, reviewed sources;
2. record real contributors and confirm employment/contractor assignment and contribution authority;
3. compare the release against the background-IP register and flag reused owned/proprietary assets;
4. resolve patent/invention review before publication;
5. lock all Python, model, npm, container, OS-package, and GitHub Action versions; prefer hashes/digests;
6. generate source and container SBOMs, including transitive and operating-system packages;
7. scan licences and known vulnerabilities, then obtain approval for exceptions;
8. produce and ship `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES`/`NOTICE` material and full licence texts as required by the actual artifacts;
9. verify dataset/model/documentation provenance and permitted redistribution or model use;
10. run secret scanning and remove credentials, personal data, internal URLs, and confidential information;
11. confirm file-by-file dissemination/licence classification, repository visibility, trademark/branding permission, required EU/HEDGE acknowledgement/emblem/disclaimer, and consortium review;
12. archive the approval, SBOM, scan reports, checksums, model manifests, release tag, and distributed binaries/images.

At the audited baseline, the repository does not contain an SBOM, third-party-notices file, contribution policy, or complete dependency lock for the backend/container/model stack. The npm lock exists but is not consumed by the gateway image build; no equivalent complete Python/container vulnerability clearance can be claimed.

## 10. Recommended IPR rules to place in the handbook

These are proposed controls for manager/legal approval, not statements of current fact:

- no dependency, model, dataset, copied documentation, icon, logo, or code snippet enters a release without source, version, licence, and approver recorded;
- permissive licences may be used only after notice obligations and compatibility with the outbound release are checked;
- copyleft, source-available, non-commercial, research-only, custom model, and unknown licences require written legal approval;
- SaaS/API access terms and database/content rights are reviewed separately from client-library licences;
- every contributor confirms authority to contribute; third-party or prior-employer materials are prohibited unless registered and approved;
- background IP remains separately identified; project access and post-project exploitation rights are written explicitly;
- existing public disclosure is reviewed for invention/patent impact, and future inventions are disclosed internally before further public commits, demos, papers, videos, or deliverables;
- Apache-2.0 notices, changed-file marking, attribution, and patent/trademark clauses are respected for redistributed works;
- AI-assisted development, if used, is recorded with tool/terms, human reviewer, and provenance checks; generated output is not assumed to be rights-clear;
- personal/confidential data are excluded from prompts, logs, test fixtures, mock data, and public repositories unless specifically authorized and protected;
- IPR and licence registers are reviewed at least at each deliverable/release and whenever the stack or data source changes.
